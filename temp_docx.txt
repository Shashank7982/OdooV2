Executive Summary
This plan sets out how to deliver a custom accounting add-on for the existing Odoo 18 installation, covering standard double-entry bookkeeping, GST, sales, purchasing, and a customizable-desk product line. It replaces an earlier draft that was strong on accounting logic and diagram coverage but was missing a schedule, resourcing, a budget framework, a risk register, and independently verified rendered diagrams. This version keeps what was already correct and adds those missing pieces.
Two decisions gate everything else and should be made before Phase 1 starts:
Confirm — by direct inspection, not by re-reading a prior report — that the addon list, dependency graph, and Odoo edition described in Section 2 match the real D:\Odoo18 installation.
Decide how the customizable desk is modelled (variant, kit, manufactured BOM, or bespoke configurator), since this drives the Phase 2 estimate.
Indicative delivery is six sequential phases over roughly 17 weeks (~4 months) for a first production release covering core accounting, sales, purchase, and GST reporting, assuming a small dedicated team (see Section 12). Cost is presented as an effort framework rather than a fixed quote, since actual cost depends on your team's rates (Section 13).
1. Purpose, Scope, and Inputs
1.1 Direct request
Inspect the existing Odoo 18 installation and its current accounting-related flows and dependencies.
Produce a full implementation plan for a custom accounting add-on.
Provide architecture and dependency diagrams and UI wireframes before implementation begins.
Account for a customizable-desk product if one is added to the catalogue.
1.2 Reference material treated as business requirements
The supplied accounting knowledge base and three workflow images (SAP-style sales information-system map, sales swimlane, purchase swimlane) are treated as business/reference requirements, not as instructions to change files or data directly. Their core requirements, carried through this plan, are:
Standard double-entry accounting: transaction → validation → journal → ledger/subledger → trial balance → statements.
Sales, purchasing, payment, GST, inventory, and reporting, including the specific ₹10,000 + ₹1,800 GST sales example and ₹5,000 + ₹900 GST purchase example.
Diagrams and screen blueprints reviewable before implementation (delivered in this version as rendered images, not pseudocode — see Sections 3–5 and 8).
1.3 In scope for the first release
Core journal/ledger hardening on top of Odoo's standard account.move / account.move.line.
Sales: inquiry → quotation → order → delivery → invoice → payment, with returns, credit/debit memos.
Purchase: request → RFQ → PO → receipt (incl. backorder) → vendor bill → payment.
Indian GST: CGST + SGST for intra-state, IGST for inter-state, aggregated into GSTR-1/3B.
Reporting: trial balance, P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, aging, GST reports.
1.4 Explicitly out of scope for the first release (candidates for later phases)
Multi-currency revaluation and multi-company consolidation beyond what Odoo provides out of the box.
E-invoice / e-way bill portal filing and GST portal submission (treated as external integration boundaries).
POS integration and affiliate payout automation beyond what already exists.

2.3 Architectural rules (apply regardless of what Phase 0 finds)
Single posting authority: every business event ultimately posts through standard account.move APIs. No competing ledger engine.
No direct balance writes: balances are derived from posted move lines only.
Balanced posting invariant: every posted move must satisfy debit = credit in company currency, within Odoo's currency precision.
Period control: posting must respect Odoo lock dates and any custom period policy.
Configuration over hard-coding: accounts, taxes, journals, analytic requirements, and product categories are configurable per company.
Reversal over deletion: posted transactions are corrected by reversal, credit note, or debit note, with a full audit trail — never a silent edit or delete.
Optional modules remain optional: GST, affiliate, OCR, budget, and POS integrations must fail gracefully rather than hard-crash when absent.
3. Target Architecture
Every business event — sales, purchase, expense, bank import, or OCR capture — passes through one shared validation and accounting-policy layer before it reaches the single posting authority (Odoo's account.move / account.move.line). Ledgers, subledgers, tax, inventory valuation, assets, deferred recognition, and analytic dimensions are all views over that one posted source, feeding one reporting and export layer.
Figure 1 — Target architecture: one shared validation layer, one posting authority, many reporting views
This directly resolves a gap in the original knowledge base, which specified the accounting engine and database logic but did not specify how sales, purchase, banking, and OCR sources should be reconciled into one authority — here they explicitly are.

10. Month-End and Year-End Close Procedure
10.1 Month-end
Finish sales, purchase, payment, receipt, expense, and bank imports for the period.
Reconcile receivables and payables; review aged reports.
Complete bank reconciliation and clear suspense/unmatched items.
Post due depreciation and deferred-recognition lines.
Review stock valuation and COGS for the period.
Run Trial Balance and confirm debit equals credit.
Run General Ledger, P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, aging, and GST reports.
Post approved adjustments through controlled journal entries and rerun the adjusted Trial Balance.
Obtain controller approval and set the Odoo lock date.
Archive the close package: exports, approvals, exceptions, audit log.
10.2 Year-end
Repeat month-end controls for the full fiscal year.
Review revenue, COGS, expenses, assets, deferred balances, tax, and intercompany balances.
Post approved year-end adjustments.
Generate closing entries for profit/loss accounts into retained earnings per the chart policy.
Generate final statutory and management reports; lock the fiscal year.
Carry forward opening balances and create next year's budgets and recurring schedules.
Retain the final close package and reversal/adjustment references.
11. Explicit Sales-Invoice Validation Checklist
Before posting a sales invoice, all ten checks below must be enforced — this is the knowledge base's own validation list, unchanged, and is the acceptance basis for test scenario T01 in Section 16.
Customer exists.
Customer is active.
Product exists.
Sales account is configured and active.
Output CGST account is configured when CGST applies.
Output SGST account is configured when SGST applies (Output IGST when inter-state).
Tax rate and tax repartition are valid.
Accounting period is open.
Invoice number is unique.
Calculated taxable value, tax, and total match the document.
Failure of any check must prevent posting and leave no partial journal, ledger, tax, balance, or audit trace.
12. Implementation Phases, Backlog, and Timeline
Six sequential phases, ~17 weeks total for a first production release. Durations assume the team in Section 13 working full-time and Phase 0 confirming the Section 2 assumptions without major surprises; add 1–2 weeks of buffer per phase if that confirmation surfaces material differences.
Figure 7 — Indicative phase timeline
Phase 0 — Baseline and safety gate (2 weeks)
Freeze and document the current installed module set and database backup procedure.
Confirm Odoo version, Python environment, PostgreSQL connectivity, and enabled companies.
Run existing custom module tests and record failures without changing unrelated code.
Complete the Section 2 validation tasks and sign off the corrected current-state memo.
Produce a module-by-module Community-vs-Enterprise compatibility matrix.
Phase 1 — Core accounting hardening (3 weeks)
Centralize shared validation helpers (company, date, period, partner, tax, account, duplicate, analytic).
Review any account.move._post override for safe cooperation with all posting flows.
Add explicit balanced-entry tests at every custom entry-creation path.
Add idempotency protection to cron-generated depreciation, deferral, and recurring moves.
Add structured audit metadata: source model, source record, actor, timestamp, reversal link.
Phase 2 — Product, inventory, and customizable-desk integration (4 weeks)
Confirm the desk's product model (variant, kit, manufactured BOM, or configurator) — open decision, see Section 15.
Configure product-category valuation and income/expense accounts.
Test sales delivery, inventory valuation, COGS, returns, vendor receipt, vendor bill, credit notes.
Validate CGST/SGST vs IGST tax behaviour for domestic/inter-state sales.
Phase 3 — Operational workflows (3 weeks)
Complete invoice/bill/payment flows and reconciliation links.
Harden CSV/XLSX bank import with preview, row-level errors, duplicate detection, rollback.
Harden OCR review: file-type limits, duplicate detection, manual override, no auto-post without approval.
Validate asset creation from bill lines and disposal/transfer/revaluation flows.
Phase 4 — Reporting and statutory outputs (3 weeks)
Reconcile trial balance, GL, P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, aging, and GST totals to posted move lines.
Add report filters for company, journal, account, partner, analytic dimension, and date.
Validate GSTR-1/GSTR-3B aggregation against posted GST invoices and credit/debit notes.
Mark portal submission / e-invoice / e-way bill as external integration boundaries needing credentials and separate tests.
Phase 5 — Security, UX, and release (2 weeks)
Test Accounting User, Accounting Manager, Finance Controller, Auditor, and CFO/Admin roles separately; confirm Auditor is read-only everywhere, including custom wizards.
Confirm bank details are hidden from unauthorized roles.
Standardize menus, breadcrumbs, status bars, and error messages.
Package upgrade scripts, demo data, migration notes, rollback plan, and release checklist.
Release gate
Implementation should not begin until the Phase 0 baseline confirmation, the product-accounting decision, the tax-jurisdiction decision, and the final role matrix are all approved.

16. Open Decisions Required Before Coding
Is the customizable desk a product variant, a kit, a manufactured BOM, or a bespoke configurator?
Which companies/jurisdictions are in scope: India only, or multi-company India + others?
Should the custom suite remain a separate "Custom Accounting" app, or surface inside standard Accounting/Sales/Purchase menus?
Which features are required for the first production release versus later phases?
Are OCR, e-invoice, e-way bill, bank integrations, and GST portal filing in scope, or only preparation/reporting?
What approval limits apply to posting, payments, assets, budget changes, and reversals?
None of Phase 2 onward should be estimated in detail until decisions 1 and 2 are made — they change the size of the single largest phase.
17. Test Strategy and Traceability Scenarios
17.1 Accounting invariants (must hold at all times)
Every posted move is balanced.
No posting is possible in a locked period.
No duplicate vendor bill posts when the configured duplicate policy rejects it.
